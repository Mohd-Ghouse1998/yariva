## Product behavior to build

Add **private personal Stories** for feelings, experiences and everyday life, including text-only posts and existing supported private photo/media where safe. They are distinct from `Scene`: a Scene is a short-lived activity/availability broadcast in Discover; a private Story is shared with people the owner explicitly approves. Do not turn Scenes into private Stories, copy Scene endpoints under a new name, or allow a public fallback.

Each Story has a clear audience choice: **selected approved connections** (safe default), **all currently approved connections**, and, if useful with the existing UI, a reusable private circle. Resolve “all currently approved” at publish time; new connections never gain access to an older Story automatically. Editing an audience may only broaden access after an explicit review/confirmation. A connection is an active `Match` or an owner-approved follow relationship. Inspect for existing follow support; if absent, implement the smallest private follow-request/accept/revoke flow needed for non-dating friendship. A unilateral unapproved follow, a Like, a discovery candidate or a past match never grants access. Keep follower lists/counts private by default. No popularity streaks or engagement-ranking feed are needed.

Add the missing, clearly named **gay, bisexual and lesbian choices** in a separate, optional identity/community-preference flow; do not add them to the `ProfileGender` enum. Offer inclusive choices such as straight, gay, lesbian, bisexual, pansexual, queer, asexual, questioning, self-describe and prefer not to say, using the app's existing localization patterns and editable copy. Keep **“how I identify”** distinct from **“communities I want to connect with”** and from **“who may see this Story.”** A user may select multiple applicable labels or none; changes and removal must be possible later. These choices must not be a mandatory onboarding gate. The current `UserDiscoveryPreference.preferredGenders` contains gender choices; it is not sexuality or community membership. Never infer identity from gender, discovery preferences, swipes or matches, and never treat interest in a community as proof of belonging to it.

Optionally narrow a Story to approved connections who **privately opt into** the relevant community. Implement this consent and membership separately from discovery preferences, keep sensitive selections private by default, and provide a clear exit. Membership is self-described, not identity verification. Community choice can only narrow an explicitly approved Story audience, never open it to strangers. If community privacy, abuse controls or clear consent cannot be made safe, keep the community filter behind a disabled feature flag, finish the approved-connection slice and leave P8-05/FEATURE-I unticked with the exact gap.

Show a plain-language **“Who can see this?”** preview before publishing, with an accurate eligible-recipient count and the chosen relationship rule, without exposing other users’ private identity selections. Let the owner delete a Story and remove viewers at any time. Use one server-owned, validated Story lifetime (24 hours is the proposed default), a documented cleanup/retention policy and an explicit Story privacy setting. Do not add exact location or EXIF location to personal Stories. Use calm, generic push copy that does not reveal community membership, sexuality or intimate content on a lock screen. Reuse report/block/moderation controls. Do not promise screenshot prevention or permanent recall of content already viewed.

## Security and system design

Define and test the effective read rule once in the backend: authenticated active viewer, active/nonexpired Story, active approved relationship, explicit per-Story grant, any opted-in community restriction, and no block, revocation or account restriction. The same policy must guard feed rows, detail, counts, notifications and each media-access path. Never trust a client-provided recipient count, identity claim, audience predicate or cached “allowed” flag. Blocks, unmatches, unfollows, leaving a community and grant removal must stop future metadata/media access; document the bounded lifetime of already issued media URLs. Use a private storage path or access proxy/short-lived signed URL as appropriate, with no public object URL or cross-user cache leak. Avoid leaking existence through error details. Rate-limit abuse-prone actions and use the app’s existing auth, error, moderation and observability patterns.

Preserve Yariva’s NestJS/Prisma/PostgreSQL modular monolith and Expo/React Native app. Before schema edits, inspect current relations and choose the fewest new entities that express Story, explicit grants, and only the missing follow/community records. Use additive migrations, constraints, indexes, bounded cursor pagination, transactional grant/revoke updates and idempotent retries where needed. Keep the audience policy in one backend owner; do not duplicate it in controllers, jobs, SQL snippets and mobile. Share types through the existing contracts; validate all untrusted input. Reuse existing auth, upload/media, moderation, notification, API client, UI components, localization and semantic theme tokens. Apply DRY, KISS and YAGNI, with focused responsibilities and clear module boundaries; use SOLID principles pragmatically rather than adding interfaces/classes for their own sake. No second backend, parallel social graph, duplicate HTTP client, hardcoded profiles/recipients/URLs/credentials, scattered policy numbers, copied DTOs or speculative abstractions. Prefer installed framework and standard-library capabilities; justify any new dependency. Keep code readable and the change scoped; fewer lines alone are not proof of optimization.
